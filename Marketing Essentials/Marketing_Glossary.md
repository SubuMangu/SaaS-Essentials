# Marketing Glossary

SaaS and software-industry terms for market research.

A compact reference of the terms used to read market reports, size an opportunity, compare competitors, and follow public SaaS companies. Definitions are written for a founder who is new to the category — short enough to scan, precise enough to use.

## 1. What SaaS sits inside

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SaaS | Software as a Service — software used in a browser or app, hosted by the vendor, paid on subscription or usage. |
| IaaS | Infrastructure as a Service — raw compute, storage, and networking (e.g. AWS EC2). |
| PaaS | Platform as a Service — tools to build applications on (e.g. Heroku). |
| On-premise | Software installed on the customer's own servers. The model SaaS largely replaced. |
| Multi-tenant | Many customers share one system. Standard, lower-cost SaaS architecture. |
| Single-tenant | One customer gets an isolated instance. Common in regulated industries. |
| API / integration | How other software talks to yours. Buyers often choose tools that plug into existing stacks. |

## 2. How the market is sliced

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| B2B / B2C / B2B2C | Sell to companies / consumers / companies that serve consumers. |
| SMB | Small and midsize businesses. Faster deals, lower prices, usually higher churn. |
| Mid-market | Companies roughly tens to a few hundred employees. |
| Enterprise | Large companies. Long sales cycles, big contracts, security reviews. |
| Horizontal SaaS | Useful across industries (CRM, email, project tools). Huge market, heavy competition. |
| Vertical SaaS | Built for one industry (dental, construction, legal). Smaller TAM, often easier to win. |
| Category | The shelf the product sits on (CRM, ERP, HCM, cybersecurity). Research is done at this level. |
| Prosumer | A professional or freelancer buying like a consumer. |
| Wedge / beachhead | The first narrow use case or customer type you attack before expanding. |

## 3. Market sizing

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| TAM | Total Addressable Market — revenue if every possible buyer purchased at your price. Theoretical ceiling. |
| SAM | Serviceable Addressable Market — the part of TAM you can serve with this product, price, geography, and regulation. |
| SOM | Serviceable Obtainable Market — the slice of SAM you can realistically win in 1–3 years. |
| Top-down sizing | Start from an analyst total and take a percentage. Fast, easy to inflate. |
| Bottom-up sizing | Count ICP companies × ACV. More credible for planning. |
| ARPU / ARPA | Average revenue per user or per account. Turns customer count into market dollars. |
| ACV | Annual Contract Value — what one customer pays per year. |
| TCV | Total Contract Value — full multi-year contract, not one year. |
| CAGR | Compound Annual Growth Rate — smoothed yearly market growth. |
| Wallet share | How much of a buyer's software budget you capture versus other tools. |

## 4. Who you sell to

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ICP | Ideal Customer Profile — the company type that gets the most value and pays well (industry, size, stack, pain). |
| Buyer persona | The human who buys or uses the product (office manager, CTO, founder). |
| Economic buyer | The person who signs the check. |
| Champion | The internal advocate pushing for your product. |
| End user | The person who lives in the product daily. |
| JTBD | Jobs to Be Done — the outcome the customer hires software for, not the feature list. |
| Pain / urgency / budget | A market is a group that feels the pain now and already spends money or time on a workaround. |

## 5. Pricing models

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Subscription | Recurring monthly or annual fee. Default SaaS model. |
| Per-seat / per-user | Price × number of users. Simple; buyers dislike unused seats. |
| Tiered / feature-based | Starter / Pro / Business / Enterprise with different features and limits. |
| Usage-based | Pay for consumption (API calls, storage, AI credits). Bills can spike. |
| Hybrid pricing | Base subscription plus usage. Common for AI features. |
| Freemium | Forever-free limited product plus paid upgrades. Conversion is often only a few percent. |
| Free trial | Time-boxed access, then the customer must pay. |
| Outcome-based | Price tied to a result delivered, not seats or raw usage. |

## 6. Go-to-market

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| GTM | Go-to-Market — who you sell to, through which channel, at what price, with what motion. |
| PLG | Product-Led Growth — the product sells itself via signup, trial, and in-app upgrade. |
| SLG | Sales-Led Growth — reps demo, negotiate, and close. Fits expensive deals. |
| Hybrid-led | Self-serve at the bottom; sales when usage or company size crosses a line. |
| Inbound | Customers find you (SEO, content, product). |
| Outbound | You find customers (email, LinkedIn, calls). |
| Sales cycle | Time from first contact to paid contract. |
| Land and expand | Win a small first deal, then grow seats, modules, or usage inside the account. |

## 7. Core SaaS metrics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MRR | Monthly Recurring Revenue — normalized monthly subscription revenue. Exclude one-time fees. |
| ARR | Annual Recurring Revenue — MRR × 12. Headline B2B number. |
| Net New MRR | New + expansion − contraction − churn. True monthly growth. |
| Logo churn | Percent of customers who leave. |
| Revenue churn | Percent of revenue that leaves. |
| NRR / NDR | Net Revenue (or Dollar) Retention — do existing customers spend more or less a year later? Above 100% means the base grows alone. |
| GRR | Gross Revenue Retention — same idea as NRR, but no credit for expansion. |
| CAC | Customer Acquisition Cost — fully loaded sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers. |
| LTV | Lifetime Value — what a customer is worth before they leave. Roughly ARPU ÷ churn rate. |
| LTV:CAC | Rule of thumb: about 3:1 or better is healthy. |
| CAC payback | Months to earn back what you spent to acquire the customer. |
| Gross margin | Revenue minus cost to deliver, as a percent. Strong SaaS is often ~70%+. |
| Burn / runway | How fast cash is spent, and how many months of cash remain. |
| Rule of 40 | Growth rate % + profit margin % ≥ 40. Later-stage health check. |

## 8. Product and validation

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MVP | Minimum Viable Product — smallest version that tests whether anyone cares. |
| PMF | Product–Market Fit — customers keep paying and telling others. |
| Activation | The moment a new user first gets value. Different from signup. |
| Cohort | A group of customers who started in the same week or month, tracked together. |
| Retention | Whether users come back and keep paying. |
| Switching cost | How painful it is to leave the current tool. |
| Incumbent | The established product already sitting in the account. |
| Build vs buy vs status quo | Real competitors include spreadsheets and doing nothing, not only branded SaaS. |
| Moat | Why a winner stays winning: data, workflow lock-in, network effects, brand, compliance. |

## 9. Research-industry jargon

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Analyst firms | Gartner, Forrester, IDC and similar publishers of market sizes and vendor rankings. |
| Magic Quadrant / Wave | Gartner / Forrester maps of vendors in a category. |
| Category creation | Trying to name a new market. Hard; most first products enter an existing shelf. |
| Commoditized category | Many similar tools, price pressure, buyers treat them as interchangeable. |
| Penetration | Share of possible buyers who already use some solution. |
| Bundling / unbundling | Suites absorb point tools; startups peel one job out of a suite. |
| AI-native / agentic | Product built around AI or agents, often priced on usage or outcomes. |

## 10. IPO and public markets

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| IPO | Initial Public Offering — first sale of shares to the public and listing on an exchange. |
| Private vs public | Private ownership is founders/funds; public companies disclose financials and anyone can buy the stock. |
| Ticker | Short trading code (CRM, HUBS, NOW). |
| S-1 / prospectus | SEC registration filing before an IPO. Rich source of market, competitor, and risk detail. |
| SEC / EDGAR | US regulator and its free public filing database. |
| 10-K / 10-Q / 8-K | Annual report / quarterly report / material-event filing after IPO. |
| Underwriters | Banks that price the IPO, find buyers, and run the roadshow. |
| Roadshow | Management's investor pitch tour before pricing. |
| Offer price / pop | IPO sale price versus the first public trade. |
| Primary vs secondary | New shares fund the company; secondary shares cash out existing holders. |
| Market cap | Share price × shares outstanding. |
| Float | Shares actually trading in public hands. |
| Lockup | Usually 90–180 days when insiders cannot sell after IPO. |
| Quiet period | Window when the company and bankers cannot hype the stock beyond the prospectus. |
| Greenshoe | Option for underwriters to sell extra shares to stabilize early trading. |
| Direct listing | Goes public without a classic underwritten IPO. |
| SPAC | Shell company that IPOs first, then merges with a private firm to take it public. |
| Follow-on offering | Selling more shares after the company is already public. |
| Comps / multiples | Public peers used as a yardstick; value often expressed as a multiple of revenue or ARR. |
| IPO window | Periods when public markets want new tech listings. |
| RPO | Remaining Performance Obligations — contracted revenue not yet recognized. |
| Guidance | Management's public forecast of future results. |

## 11. Funding path before IPO

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bootstrapped | Built mainly on customer revenue, little or no venture capital. |
| Pre-seed / seed | First outside money to find a product and first customers. |
| Series A / B / C | Larger rounds used to scale sales, product, and geography. |
| Growth equity | Late-stage capital when the model already works. |
| Unicorn | Private company valued at $1B or more. |
| Valuation | What a funding round implies the company is worth. |
| Dilution | Founder ownership shrinks as new shares are issued. |
| Cap table | Who owns how much of the company. |
| Exit | How investors get cash: IPO, acquisition, or a private share sale. |
| M&A | Merger and acquisition — a company is bought. More common than IPO for most SaaS firms. |

## How to use this list

Start with the job/pain and ICP, name the category, list alternatives (including spreadsheets), size SAM then SOM, note pricing and GTM, then look for retention clues. Public filings (S-1, 10-K) are a free research library — you do not need to plan an IPO to read them.
